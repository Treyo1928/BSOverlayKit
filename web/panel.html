Sound

The music's level on a scene is the first slider times that scene's own (Getting Ready starts at 50 %: a breather before the song); the scene on air is marked. The same figures sound the same on every scene. Hitsounds have their own slider and do not follow the music's. 100 % is the replay as recorded; it cannot go louder. Sliders act as you drag; a scene change fades. The preview on this page shows the scene with the video boxes dark and makes no sound, so an open panel costs nothing. Tick "Replays and sound in this preview" to have it draw the replays and play their sound here (this browser only, and only while this page is open).

Video holes

Filled automatically: each player's live TA replay during a match, the replay playlist in the big box. Only type a link here to override one.
How wide every replay's first-person view is, and where the camera sits along the track: behind the player's head (left) or in front of it (right). Forward and back are the track's, so the camera does not swing when a player turns their head. A wider view shows more of the lanes and the sabers at the edges, and makes the notes look smaller and faster. Both change on stream as you drag.
For players who look far up or down while playing: the replay's first-person camera stays between these two limits (0 and 0 = always level; left/right still follows them). Applies to every replay at once. To see a limit, press its button, or just change its number: every replay on screen looks that far up or down for a few seconds, then follows its player again. That shows on stream too, so set it up before going live.
Whatever window or monitor the overlay is on: a 4K screen at 150 % would otherwise draw 1.5× the pixels per side (2.25× the work) for the same 1080p stream.
OBS's Browser Source on Linux with an NVIDIA card draws the replays on the CPU: lower these until OBS runs smoothly. Paused replays nearly stop drawing.
ChroViewer's quality setting is the floor's reflection: High draws it at 2048 px and smoothed, Medium at 1024, Low at 512 with fewer things reflected; Mirror off skips the reflection pass, which saves the most. A change redraws each replay's scene (a short blink). Distortion is the heat-haze effect some maps use on walls and notes.
